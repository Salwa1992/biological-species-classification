# Literature Review

## 1. Random Forests for Regression and Classification (Breiman, 2001)
This foundational paper introduces the Random Forest algorithm and demonstrates its robustness against overfitting. The author explains how combining multiple decision trees through bagging and feature randomness significantly improves prediction accuracy. It highlights the model's ability to handle complex interactions and non-linear relationships without intensive hyperparameter tuning.

## 2. Applied Predictive Modeling (Kuhn & Johnson, 2013)
This text provides a practical blueprint for the entire predictive modeling lifecycle, from problem definition to evaluation. It stresses the importance of choosing evaluation metrics that align with business goals rather than relying solely on accuracy. The authors demonstrate how cross-validation ensures that performance metrics remain realistic and generalizable to unseen data.

## 3. Data Preprocessing for Machine Learning (Garcia et al., 2015)
This comprehensive write-up details the critical impact of data cleaning and feature engineering on model performance. The authors analyze various techniques for handling missing values, scaling features, and encoding categorical variables. The paper concludes that proper preprocessing often yields higher performance gains than switching to more complex algorithms.
